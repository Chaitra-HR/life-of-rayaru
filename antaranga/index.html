Bhuvanagiri · 1595ವೆಂಕಟನಾಥ

Before sannyāsa, Rayaru was known as Venkatanatha.

Born into a family of scholars and musicians, Venkatanatha grew through study, music, teaching and a household life marked by severe hardship.

1621Sannyāsa, Tanjore

Bhuvanagiri · the first lessonಅಕ್ಷರಾಭ್ಯಾಸ

His schooling began, as every child’s does, with the Praṇava.

During Venkanna’s Aksharabhyasa, his father Thimmanna Bhatta wrote Omkara and explained that it represented the Supreme Lord. The young Venkatanatha looked at the syllable and asked how something so small could express Lord Narayana, whose auspicious attributes are infinite.

Those gathered were astonished to hear such a question from a child.

Madurai · the householdಮಧುರೈ

After his father’s passing, Venkatanatha continued his studies under Lakshminarasimhacharya. He later married Saraswati Bai, and they had a son, Lakshminarayana.

Life, however, was not easy. Venkatanatha devoted himself to teaching and learning, without asking for anything in return. The family often had very little to live on.

Kumbakonam · the Mathaಕುಂಭಕೋಣ

The search for deeper learning led him to Kumbakonam.

Under Sri Sudhindra Tirtha, Venkatanatha immersed himself in deeper study. His command of Vyākaraṇa earned him the title Mahābhāṣyācārya, marking a new stage in the scholar he was becoming.

Kumbakonam · the dream

Sri Sudheendra Tirtha was searching for a successor.

In a dream, Moola Rama gave him a name: Venkatanatha. But Venkatanatha was still a grihastha, with duties he could not simply leave behind.

Then came a dream of his own. Vidya Lakshmi appeared to him and directed him towards sannyāsa. By morning, the path ahead was clear.

Tanjore · 1621 · the sannyāsa

After his son’s upanayana, Venkatanatha came to Tanjore. There, under Sri Sudheendra Tirtha, he entered sannyāsa.

Venkatanatha

Sri Raghavendra Tirtha

The name had already been indicated by Moola Rama. The scholar and teacher remained. But his life now continued in sannyāsa, devoted to śāstra, teaching and the worship of Moola Rama.

The writing yearsಪರಿಮಳಾಚಾರ್ಯ

Around 45 works, written with remarkable clarity.

Rayaru wrote extensively on the works of Madhvacharya, Jayatirtha and the wider Tattvavāda tradition, clarifying difficult passages and carrying their arguments forward.

Here are five of those granthas, as they are known today.

On Jayatirtha’s Nyāya Sudhā

Nyāya Sudhā Parimaḷa

Nyāya Sudhā is one of the most demanding works in Tattvavāda. Rayaru opens up its arguments step by step, showing not only what Jayatirtha says, but how the reasoning holds together. This work earned him the name Parimaḷācārya.

On the Brahma Sūtras

Tantra Dīpikā

A guide to the Brahma Sūtras through the Madhwa tradition. Rayaru brings together the sūtras, their arguments and their connections, helping the reader see the larger structure behind them.

Mīmāṃsā

Bhaṭṭa Saṅgraha

Before scripture can be explained, one question comes first: how do we know what a Vedic sentence means? Rayaru examines the rules of interpretation, injunction and meaning that shape how the Veda is read.

The Ṛgveda

Mantrārtha Mañjarī

Rayaru explains the first forty sūktas of the Ṛgveda, following the path laid by Madhvacharya. He draws out the meaning of the mantras and shows how they are understood within the larger vision of Tattvavāda.

On Jayatirtha’s Tattva Prakāśikā

Tattva Prakāśikā Bhāvadīpa

Jayatirtha explains Madhvacharya’s Brahma Sūtra Bhāṣya. Rayaru takes that explanation further, unpacking difficult arguments and making the reasoning behind Madhva’s interpretation easier to follow.

Tattvavāda · the siddhāntaತತ್ತ್ವವಾದ

To understand Rayaru’s work, we have to enter the world he was explaining.

Look again at the river. The water is not the stone. The stone is not the tree. One jīva is not another. And none of them is Sri Hari. Yet none exists independently of Him. This is one of the foundations of Tattvavāda: difference is not something to be overcome. It is real. Madhvacharya described five such differences as pañca-bheda.

The river gives us a way to see them.

Sri Hari · jīvaಪಂಚಭೇದ

The moon, and its reflection.

The reflection depends on the moon, but it never becomes the moon. In the same way, the jīva depends on Sri Hari and remains eternally distinct from Him.

Sri Hari · jaḍa

The moon is not the stone it lights.

The stone is real, but it does not know or act for itself. It is jaḍa: matter without consciousness. Sri Hari is distinct from matter, and matter remains dependent on Him.

jīva · jīva

One reflection is not another.

Every jīva is a distinct self. One soul never becomes another. Even in mokṣa, its individuality remains.

jīva · jaḍa

The self is not the body.

The jīva is the one who knows and experiences. The body it lives through is matter. They belong together in this life, but they are not the same.

jaḍa · jaḍa

The stone is not the water.

Both are matter. Yet one thing does not become another simply because both are jaḍa. Difference exists even within the material world.

Hari Sarvottama

Everything here is real. But only one is independent.

The jīva is real. The world is real. The difference between them is real. Yet neither stands on its own.

Sri Hari alone is svatantra. All else exists in dependence on Him.

And this was not a siddhānta Rayaru kept within books.

The road · 1621 to 1671ದಾರಿ

He travelled for fifty years, teaching and writing.

At Madurai, his command of Mīmāṃsā was recognised through Bhaṭṭa Saṅgraha. At Udupi, he stayed before Sri Krishna, teaching and completing important granthas.

Along the way came stories of grace too. At Kiratagiri, tradition remembers Rayaru reviving a child who had died by apamṛtyu.

Manchale · 1671ಮಂಚಾಲೆ

At Manchale, the journey came to rest.

When the Nawab of Adoni offered Rayaru land, he asked for Manchale. Venkanna urged him to choose a more fertile village, but Rayaru would have no other. He said this ground had already been sanctified by Prahlada’s yajña, and Manchalamma was his kula-devata. This was the place he chose for his Brindavana.

In 1671, on the banks of the Tungabhadra, Rayaru prepared to enter it.
